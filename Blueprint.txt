01
The solution in one page

Most teams will read this problem statement and build a classifier: feed in food properties, predict a material. That is the wrong shape of solution and it will not survive contact with a real user.

Three reasons. First, there is no labelled dataset of "food → correct packaging" large enough to train on, so a model would be learning someone's spreadsheet. Second, packaging selection is governed by physics (permeation, sorption, respiration) and by law (FSSAI, BIS, PWM Rules) — both of which are knowable exactly, so approximating them with a neural net throws away precision for no gain. Third, the user who most needs this tool cannot supply the model's inputs. A farmer does not know their potato's water activity.

Aavaran AI is built the other way round. A deterministic, auditable engine does the deciding. AI does the two things AI is genuinely good at: filling in what the user didn't say, and explaining the result in their language.
Resolve

Turn whatever the user gave you — a photo, a crop name, a voice note in Marathi — into a complete parameter set, with explicit uncertainty on every value you had to guess.
Decide

Derive barrier targets from first principles, filter by law, rank candidate structures on cost / shelf-life / recyclability. Every number traceable to a standard or an equation.
Explain

Render the same result three ways: a picture and one sentence for a farmer, a spec sheet and RFQ for a startup, a full model dump for a researcher.
The single most important design decision

The system must be allowed to say "packaging will not save this." A cut potato at 30 °C ambient has no packaging solution — any film that slows moisture loss also traps condensation and accelerates microbial growth, and a low-oxygen pack at abuse temperature is a Clostridium botulinum hazard. A tool that always produces a confident material recommendation is a tool that will eventually poison someone. Aavaran AI routes that case to: chill it, sell it within N hours, or process it. This refusal capability is what makes the rest of the advice credible.

02
System architecture

One engine, ten stages, three front doors. Everything downstream of stage 2 is deterministic and unit-tested. Stages 1 and 10 are the only places a language model touches the flow, and neither can change a number.
1
Input Parser

Free text, voice, photo, or form. LLM + fuzzy matcher maps to a commodity ID, a physical state, and any explicit parameters. Outputs strict JSON against a schema. Confidence per field.
2
Commodity Profile Resolver

Hierarchical lookup: cultivar → commodity → group → physicochemical class. Never fails; degrades to a wider band with lower confidence. Produces every missing parameter with a source tag.
3
State Adjuster

Applies modifiers for cut/whole, hours since harvest, ripeness stage, washed, bruised, blanched. Adjusts respiration rate, water activity, microbial risk class, and consumes elapsed shelf-life budget.
4
Environment Resolver

Storage temperature and RH from the user, or from geolocation + month climatology, or from the storage-type default (ambient / chilled / frozen). Models the worst leg of the journey, not the average.
5
Requirement Derivation

The physics. Produces a requirement vector: max WVTR, max OTR (or min OTR for produce), gauge, seal strength, puncture, light barrier OD, target gas mix, anti-fog need.
6
Material Matcher

Hard-filter the film/structure database on the requirement vector plus process constraints (FFS machine, retort, microwave, metal detection). Then score and rank.
7
Compliance Filter

Law-as-data rules for the destination market. Blocks, warnings, and required documents. Nothing reaches the user that is illegal where they will sell it.
8
Shelf-Life Simulator

Runs all four failure-mode models per surviving candidate. Reports the shortest and names the mechanism. Returns a curve, not just a number.
9
Cost & Sustainability Optimizer

₹/pack, ₹ per kg of food protected, EPR category and liability, recyclability. Emits the Pareto frontier so the user sees what an extra rupee buys.
10
Explanation Renderer

Takes the sealed result object and renders it for the active mode and language. The LLM writes prose around numbers it is forbidden to alter.
Hard architectural rule

The engine returns a sealed result object — a JSON document with every input, assumption, intermediate value, equation ID, and citation. The UI and the LLM are pure functions of that object. This means you can replay any recommendation months later, diff two engine versions against the same input, and hand a regulator a complete audit trail. Build this on day one; retrofitting provenance is impossible.
Where machine learning actually earns its place

Being precise about this is a differentiator, not a weakness. Three legitimate ML jobs:
Perception

A MobileNetV3/EfficientNet-Lite classifier on-device (ONNX) identifies commodity, ripeness stage, and visible defects from a phone photo. Trained on public sets (Fruits-360, PlantVillage-style, plus your own field captures). This removes the hardest input barrier for a farmer.
Resolution

A sentence-transformer embedding index maps "aloo", "batata", "potato bora" to the same commodity node. An LLM handles messy free text into schema-valid JSON. Both are matching problems, which is what these models are for.
Calibration

A gradient-boosted residual model that corrects the physics prediction using logged real outcomes. Starts as identity (zero data, zero harm) and improves regionally. Never replaces the physics — it adds a bounded correction term with its own confidence interval.

Everything else — the decision itself — is deterministic. When a judge, a food-safety officer, or a customer asks "why this film?", the answer is an equation and a regulation, not a weight matrix.

Respiration class drives everything for fresh produce. Seed from USDA Agriculture Handbook 66 (The Commercial Storage of Fruits, Vegetables, and Florist and Nursery Stocks) — it is public-domain, expert-authored, and contains respiration rates at five temperatures, ethylene production, chilling sensitivity, and optimum storage conditions for ~140 commodities. That single handbook is the backbone of your produce KB.

04
The inference engine — and the sparse-input problem

"Potato, cut two days ago" is the hardest and most important case in the brief. Here is exactly how the engine handles it.
4.1 · Hierarchical fallback

The resolver never returns "unknown". It walks a ladder and reports how far down it had to go:
L0
Measured

User supplied the value. Confidence 1.0. Used as-is.
L1
Cultivar

"Kufri Jyoti potato" → cultivar-specific record. Confidence ~0.9, narrow band.
L2
Commodity

"Potato" → species record from AH-66 + IFCT. Confidence ~0.8, band = observed cultivar spread.
L3
Group

"Root/tuber" → group medians. Confidence ~0.6, wide band.
L4
Physicochemical class

"High-moisture starchy, cut surface, low-acid" → class envelope. Confidence ~0.4. The engine now designs to the worst case in the band and says so loudly.

Two rules make this safe. First, design to the conservative end of the band: if respiration might be 8–24 mg CO₂/kg·h, size the perforation for 24, because an over-breathable pack loses quality slowly while an under-breathable pack ferments fast. Second, propagate uncertainty through the models — run the shelf-life simulation over the parameter band (Monte Carlo, 2,000 draws) and report P10/P50/P90, not a single number. "7 days (likely 5–9)" is a far more useful and far more honest output than "7 days".
4.2 · State modifiers

The difference between a potato and a cut potato is not a different commodity — it is the same commodity with multipliers applied. Encoding this as modifiers rather than separate records keeps the KB small and the logic inspectable.
Modifier	Effect on respiration	Other effects	Engine consequence
Cut / sliced / peeled	×3 to ×7 (wound response)	Exposed surface → enzymatic browning; microbial risk jumps to high; cut surface loses moisture fast	Forces chilled branch; adds anti-browning step; adds botulinum interlock; adds anti-fog
Hours since harvest/cut	Declining but elevated	Consumes shelf-life budget	Subtract elapsed equivalent-days via Q₁₀ before predicting remaining life
Ripeness stage	Climacteric peak ×2–3	Ethylene autocatalysis	May add ethylene scavenger (KMnO₄ sachet) to recommendation
Washed / hydro-cooled	—	Free surface moisture → condensation	Mandatory anti-fog film; increase perforation; warn about drying before packing
Bruised / mechanically damaged	×1.3 to ×2	Ethylene ×2–4; rot entry point	Downgrade predicted life; recommend sorting before packing
Cured (onion, potato, sweet potato)	×0.6 to ×0.9	Suberised wound periderm	Extends life 1.5–3×; unlocks ventilated mesh/jute instead of film
Blanched / thermally treated	→ 0	Enzymes inactivated; becomes a non-respiring product	Switch entirely to barrier regime
4.3 · Worked example A — "Potato, cut 2 days ago, Mumbai"

Input: commodity = potato; state = cut; time since cut = 48 h; location = Mumbai (19.08 N, 72.88 E), September; storage = "the shed"; destination = local market.

Resolver output (L2 + modifiers): intact potato respiration ≈ 3.5 mg CO₂/kg·h at 5 °C (AH-66). Environment Resolver pulls Mumbai September climatology → 28–31 °C, 78–85 % RH. Arrhenius with Ea ≈ 58 kJ/mol scales 5 °C → 30 °C, roughly ×8. Cut modifier ×3–7. Effective respiration ≈ 84–196 mg CO₂/kg·h — an extremely-high-respiration product in a hot, humid environment.

Failure-mode race: microbial growth model (low-acid, pH 6.1, aw 0.98, 30 °C) predicts spoilage in 6–14 hours. Enzymatic browning is visible in 1–3 hours. Moisture and oxidation models are irrelevant at this timescale. The shortest mechanism wins and is named.

Engine verdict: packaging is not the limiting factor. The system refuses to recommend a sealed film and instead outputs, in farmer mode:

⚠ Cut potato kept 2 days in the heat is not safe to sell.

No bag or wrap can fix this. If it smells sour, is slimy, or has grey/dark patches, discard it.

Then it gives the forward-looking advice — what to do next time, which is the actual value:

    Don't cut until you sell. Whole cured potato at 7–10 °C and 90–95 % RH keeps for months; cut potato keeps for hours. Cutting destroys ~99 % of the shelf life.
    If you must sell cut potato: dip in cold water with a little lemon juice and salt (citric + ionic anti-browning), drain fully, keep on ice or at ≤5 °C, pack in a perforated 25–40 µm LDPE or PP bag with anti-fog, and sell the same day.
    Never seal cut potato airtight at room temperature. A sealed bag with no air is dangerous, not fresher.
    Whole potato packaging: ventilated jute or 5 kg woven PP mesh, kept dark. Light turns potato green and bitter — that green is a toxin, and an opaque sack is a safety measure.

4.4 · Worked example B — "Besan namkeen, want 6 months, selling across Maharashtra"

Resolver: fried snack class → aw ≈ 0.25, fat ≈ 28 %, critical aw for crispness ≈ 0.45, oxidation-sensitive (unsaturated fat), light-sensitive.

Two failure modes race. Moisture model (Labuza with GAB isotherm) at 30 °C / 75 % RH for 180 days on a 100 g pack with 0.055 m² area gives a required WVTR ≤ 0.6 g/m²·day. Oxidation model with a peroxide-value budget gives a required OTR ≤ 1.2 cc/m²·day. Both must be met simultaneously, so the binding spec is the stricter of each dimension.

05
The maths that must be right

Four failure-mode models run in parallel for every candidate package. The predicted shelf life is the minimum across them, and the winning mechanism is reported by name. Implement each as a pure function with property tests.
5.1 · Moisture-limited (dry foods: biscuits, namkeen, powders, spices)

Water vapour permeates the film until the food reaches its critical water activity — the point where a biscuit goes soft or a powder cakes. The classical treatment couples the film's permeance to the food's moisture sorption isotherm.

Steady-state moisture flux into the pack:

    dm/dt = (WVTR_film / Δp_test) · A · (p_out − p_in)

With a LINEAR isotherm (m = b·a_w + c), valid up to a_w ≈ 0.5, this
integrates to a closed form:

    ln[ (m_e − m_i) / (m_e − m_c) ]  =  (k/x)·(A / W_s)·(p_0 / b)·t

    m_e = equilibrium moisture at external RH      (kg water / kg solids)
    m_i = initial moisture,  m_c = critical moisture
    k/x = film permeance                            (g / m²·day·kPa)
    A   = pack surface area (m²),  W_s = dry solids mass (kg)
    p_0 = saturation vapour pressure at storage T   (kPa)
    b   = isotherm slope
    t   = shelf life (days)

Rearranged for DESIGN — the spec the engine actually emits:

    permeance_max = (W_s · b) / (A · p_0 · t) · ln[(m_e−m_i)/(m_e−m_c)]

    → convert to a quotable WVTR at 38 °C / 90 % RH (ASTM F1249)

Above aw ≈ 0.5 the linear isotherm under-predicts moisture uptake and therefore over-predicts shelf life — the dangerous direction. Use the GAB isotherm there; a closed-form t(aw) solution for GAB now exists in the literature, avoiding numerical integration. Comparative work shows GAB gives the most consistent and stable estimates of time-to-critical-aw, with the linear model acceptable only when its fit covers the low-aw range including the critical limit.
Implementation guidance: fit GAB constants (m₀, C_g, K) per food class and store them in the KB. Use the linear model only when a_w,crit < 0.5, and have the engine log which isotherm it used.
5.2 · Oxidation-limited (fats, nuts, fried snacks, milk powder, ghee)

Give the product an OXYGEN BUDGET: the total mass of O₂ it can absorb
before peroxide value / hexanal crosses the sensory threshold.

    Q_budget  [mg O₂ per kg product]   ← from the KB, per fat class
                                          (typical 5–30 mg/kg for
                                           unsaturated fried snacks)

    O₂ available = residual headspace O₂ + ingress through film

    ingress(t) = OTR_film · A · Δp(O₂) · t        [cc]
               → × 1.43 mg/cc  to convert to mass

    OTR_max = (Q_budget · M_product − O₂_headspace) / (1.43 · A · Δp · t)

Practical consequence, worth hardcoding as a sanity check:
    for 6–12 month ambient shelf life on an oxidation-sensitive food,
    you need OTR ≤ ~1 cc/m²·day AND WVTR ≤ ~0.5 g/m²·day
    → metallized PET/PE, AlOx-PET/PE, or foil laminate. Nothing else.

Nitrogen flush matters more than people expect: reducing residual
headspace O₂ from 21 % to 1 % can dominate the total budget for a
short-to-medium shelf life. Recommend it BEFORE recommending a
more expensive film.

5.3 · Respiration-limited (fresh produce MAP) — the core produce maths

At equilibrium, oxygen entering through the film exactly equals oxygen consumed by the produce. Set the two equal and solve for the film you need.

O₂ balance at steady state:

    OTR_req · A · (y_out − y_in)  =  R_O2 · M · 24

    OTR_req  required film O₂ transmission  [mL / m²·day·atm]
    A        pack surface area              [m²]
    y_out    external O₂ fraction           = 0.209 atm
    y_in     TARGET internal O₂ fraction    e.g. 0.05 atm
    R_O2     respiration rate               [mL O₂ / kg·h]
    M        product mass                   [kg]

    →  OTR_req = (R_O2 · M · 24) / (A · (y_out − y_in))

CO₂ balance, same form:

    CO2TR_req = (R_CO2 · M · 24) / (A · (y_in,CO2 − y_out,CO2))

THE SELECTIVITY PROBLEM — this is the real design decision:

    β = P_CO2 / P_O2

    Continuous polymer films:  β ≈ 3 – 6   (CO₂ dissolves far better)
    Perforations (gas in air): β ≈ 0.78    (ratio of diffusivities,
                                            D_CO2 0.160 / D_O2 0.205 cm²/s)

    Compute β_required from your target O₂ and CO₂ simultaneously.
    If β_req ≈ 3–6  → a continuous film can hit both targets.
    If β_req ≈ 1    → you MUST use micro-perforation.
    This single comparison is the breathable-vs-barrier branch point.

Temperature scaling of respiration (Arrhenius):

    R(T) = R_ref · exp[ (Ea/Rg) · (1/T_ref − 1/T) ]      T in Kelvin
    Ea for common fruit & veg in air: ~29 – 93 kJ/mol
    Or the field-friendly form: R(T) = R_ref · Q10^((T−T_ref)/10),
    Q10 ≈ 2–3

Micro-perforation sizing (Fick through the hole, with end correction):

    N = (R_O2 · M · L_eff) / (D_O2 · A_hole · Δc_O2)

    A_hole = π d² / 4        typical d = 50–200 µm (laser drilled)
    L_eff  = L + 1.18 r      L = film thickness, r = hole radius
    Δc_O2  = (y_out − y_in) · P / (Rg · T)

    Report N, d, and their placement. Always round UP on N.

CLAMPS (non-negotiable):
    y_in(O₂)  ≥ 0.02          fermentation + botulinum floor
    y_in(CO₂) ≤ commodity CO₂ injury threshold from KB
    If no (film, N, d) satisfies both within the clamps →
    return "no passive MAP solution; use active flush or vented packaging"

Sanity anchor for your unit tests: published modelling of fresh-cut pineapple found target O₂ and CO₂ transmission rates of roughly 7,300–12,500 and 13,900–23,500 mL/m²·day·atm respectively, covering intrinsic and extrinsic variability. If your solver returns values in that ballpark for a similar high-respiration fresh-cut product, your maths is wired correctly.
5.4 · Microbially-limited (chilled, high-aw, cut, protein)

Use a square-root (Ratkowsky/Belehrádek) growth-rate model for the
relevant spoilage organism or pathogen, parameterised from ComBase:

    √μ_max = b · (T − T_min)  ·  f(a_w) · f(pH) · f(CO₂)

    Time to spoilage = (log10 N_spoil − log10 N_0) / (μ_max / ln10)
                       + lag phase

    CO₂ in MAP is bacteriostatic — include the CO₂ inhibition term or
    you will systematically UNDER-predict MAP shelf life.

SAFETY BRANCH — runs before, and independently of, quality:
    IF a_w > 0.92 AND pH > 4.6 AND O₂_target < 2 % AND T_max > 5 °C:
        → HARD BLOCK. C. botulinum hazard.
        → Message: "This combination is unsafe. Either guarantee
          ≤5 °C throughout, or do not remove oxygen."

Quality prediction and SAFETY prediction are separate outputs.
Never let a good quality number mask a safety failure.

5.5 · Putting it together

def predict_shelf_life(profile, env, package):
    results = []
    if profile.a_w < 0.6:
        results.append(moisture_model(profile, env, package))
    if profile.fat_pct > 3 or profile.oxidation_sensitive:
        results.append(oxidation_model(profile, env, package))
    if profile.respiring:
        results.append(respiration_model(profile, env, package))
    if profile.a_w > 0.85:
        results.append(microbial_model(profile, env, package))

    safety = safety_gate(profile, env, package)     # separate, blocking
    limiting = min(results, key=lambda r: r.days)

    return ShelfLife(
        days_p50   = limiting.days,
        days_p10   = monte_carlo_p10(results, profile.bands),
        days_p90   = monte_carlo_p90(results, profile.bands),
        mechanism  = limiting.name,       # "moisture uptake", "rancidity"...
        all_modes  = results,             # show the race, not just winner
        safety     = safety,              # PASS / WARN / BLOCK
        equations  = [r.equation_id for r in results],
        confidence = aggregate_conf(profile, env),
    )

Reporting which mechanism limits shelf life is what converts a number into an action. "90 days, limited by moisture uptake" tells a manufacturer to improve the moisture barrier or add a desiccant. "90 days, limited by rancidity" tells them to flush with nitrogen or add an antioxidant. The same number, two completely different fixes.

Three user modes, one engine

The brief asks for a tool that a farmer, a startup founder and a packaging technologist can all trust. That does not mean three products. It means one engine and three renderings of the same sealed result object — plus three different amounts of work the resolver has to do.
KISAN MODE

For: farmers, FPO members, mandi traders, fruit & vegetable sellers.

Input: three taps maximum. Photo or crop picker → state (whole / cut / washed, days since harvest via a slider with pictures) → destination (sell today / cold store / send by truck).

Output: one big card. A picture of the right bag or crate. One sentence of instruction. Three "do not do this" icons. A price and where to buy locally.

Non-negotiables: works offline, works on a 2 GB Android phone, reads aloud in the local language, never shows a number with a unit the user has not met.
BUSINESS MODE

For: food startups, MSMEs, FPO processing units, cloud kitchens, D2C brands.

Input: product form, batch size, pack size, target shelf life, distribution route, budget per pack, filling machine type.

Output: three ranked structures with full spec sheets, shelf-life curves, ₹/pack, a compliance checklist for their FSSAI licence category, an EPR liability estimate, and a generated RFQ document they can email to converters verbatim.

The killer feature: the RFQ. Most small brands cannot write a film specification, so they accept whatever a converter offers. Generating a correct, standards-referenced RFQ shifts the power in that negotiation.
EXPERT MODE

For: packaging technologists, food scientists, IIP/CFTRI/NIFTEM researchers, QA managers.

Input: every parameter, directly editable. Override any assumption. Supply your own isotherm constants, your own respiration data, your own film CoA.

Output: the full model dump — every equation with its inputs, sensitivity tornado chart, Pareto frontier, Monte Carlo distribution, side-by-side scenario comparison, CSV and PDF export, and a permanent link to the sealed result object.

Why it matters: experts are the ones who will validate you publicly. Give them enough rope to check your work and they become your distribution channel.

08
Trust architecture

You are asking people to change how they handle food based on software output. The engineering that earns that right is as important as the engineering that produces the answer.
1 · Provenance on every number

Each value carries a source badge — STANDARD PEER-REVIEWED VENDOR DATA MODEL ESTIMATE — and tapping it shows the citation. No unattributed numbers anywhere in the product.
2 · Confidence, shown not hidden

A chunky segmented bar, never a percentage. Ranges, not points: "5 to 9 days" beats "7 days". Users forgive a wide range; they do not forgive a confident wrong number.
3 · The confidence gate

Below a threshold the engine does not give a single answer. It gives a conservative safe envelope, says why it is uncertain, and names the one input that would fix it. Refusing to over-claim is a feature.
4 · The assumption ledger

Everything guessed is listed and one tap from being corrected. Users who correct one value immediately understand what the system is doing and start trusting the rest.
5 · Separate safety from quality

A safety BLOCK is a different visual language entirely — full coral fill, no dismissal, no override in any mode. Quality advice is negotiable; safety is not.
6 · The outcome loop

"How did it actually go?" after the predicted shelf life elapses. Logged outcomes train the residual calibrator and, more importantly, let you publish a real accuracy figure instead of a claim.
7 · Lab referral, not lab replacement

Directory of NABL-accredited packaging labs with an auto-generated test request listing exactly which tests, which standards, which conditions, and an indicative cost. The tool tells you when it has reached its limit.
8 · Versioned and replayable

Every result records engine version, KB version and rule-set version. Any recommendation can be replayed months later and diffed against the current engine. This is what a food-safety auditor will ask for.
Say this plainly, everywhere

Aavaran AI is a decision-support tool. It does not replace validation studies, accelerated shelf-life testing, migration testing, or a NABL conformity certificate. Under the Food Safety and Standards Act the responsibility for compliance rests with the Food Business Operator. Print that in the footer, in the PDF export, and in the onboarding. Teams that hide their limitations lose trust the first time they are wrong; teams that state them survive it.
8.1 · Evaluation plan — prove it works

    Golden test set. 60–100 commodity × condition cases with published or expert-agreed correct answers, drawn from AH-66, IIP handbooks, and peer-reviewed MAP studies. Run on every commit. Report accuracy publicly in the app.
    Physics property tests. Assert the invariants: doubling area halves required permeance; raising temperature never increases predicted shelf life; MAP solver output must satisfy the mass balance it was derived from; a perfect barrier must return an infinite moisture-limited life.
    Limiting-case tests. Zero respiration must collapse to the pure-barrier case. Zero permeability must collapse to hermetic. Aluminium foil must beat every polymer on both axes.
    Expert panel. Five packaging technologists blind-score 30 recommendations on a 5-point scale. Target: median ≥4, zero unsafe recommendations. Publish the result including the failures.
    Field validation. Partner with one FPO. Twenty side-by-side batches, packed per recommendation versus current practice, measured loss at day N. This one number — "18 % loss became 7 %" — is worth more than every other metric combined.
    Adversarial testing. Deliberately feed unsafe combinations (cut melon in vacuum at ambient; low-acid vegetable in oil at room temperature) and verify a BLOCK every time.

Neo-brutalist design system

Neo-brutalism is a good fit here for a reason that is not aesthetic: it forces hierarchy. Thick borders, hard shadows and flat saturated colour make it impossible to bury a warning in soft grey text. A safety block rendered in this language cannot be missed by a user glancing at a phone in bright sunlight.
10.1 · Tokens

/* colour — flat, saturated, high contrast */
--paper:  #FFFDF2;   /* off-white ground, warmer than pure white */
--ink:    #0B0B0B;   /* borders, text, shadows — ONE black, always */
--yellow: #FFE500;   /* primary action, highlight */
--cyan:   #5CE1FF;   /* information, business mode */
--lime:   #C9FF3D;   /* success, safe, eco */
--coral:  #FF6B52;   /* danger, safety block — reserved, never decorative */
--violet: #C3A6FF;   /* expert mode, advanced */
--grey:   #E8E4D6;   /* table stripes, disabled */

/* structure — the whole system is these four lines */
--bd:      3px solid var(--ink);      /* every border, no exceptions */
--radius:  0;                          /* nothing is round */
--shadow:  6px 6px 0 var(--ink);      /* hard, never blurred */
--shadow-lg: 10px 10px 0 var(--ink);

/* type */
--font-display: "Archivo Black";       /* headings, numbers, buttons */
--font-body:    "Space Grotesk";       /* prose */
--font-mono:    "JetBrains Mono";      /* specs, units, codes */

/* motion — snap, don't ease */
--t: 80ms linear;

10.2 · Component rules
Button

3px ink border, 6px hard shadow, flat fill, uppercase Archivo Black. Hover: translate(-2px,-2px) + shadow 8px. Active: translate(4px,4px) + shadow 2px — it physically presses.

Kisan mode minimum height 64px. Assume a cracked screen and a calloused thumb.
Card

Border + shadow, never both a shadow and a background gradient. Cards may overlap deliberately with negative margin — the layered-paper look is the whole point. Nesting cards more than two deep looks like a bug; use colour fill instead.
Confidence meter

Ten discrete blocks, filled or empty, 3px border around the whole row. Never a smooth gradient bar — segmentation communicates "this is an estimate" honestly. Label it in words: LOW / FAIR / GOOD / HIGH.
Safety block

Full coral fill, ink text, 6px border, 10px shadow, an icon, and no dismiss button. Reserve coral exclusively for this. The moment coral appears somewhere decorative, the safety language is dead.
Spec table

2px internal rules, ink header row with paper text, alternating grey stripes. Numbers in JetBrains Mono, right-aligned, unit in a smaller weight. Horizontal scroll inside its own bordered container, never a page-level sideways scroll.
Mode switcher

Three chunky tabs, active one sits 3px lower with a reduced shadow and takes its mode's colour (yellow / cyan / violet). Colour-codes the entire session so the user always knows which lens they are in.
10.3 · Accessibility, non-negotiable

    Contrast ≥ 7:1 for all body text. The palette above is chosen to pass on the paper ground — check every new colour before adding it.
    Never colour alone. Every coral block carries a ⚠ icon and the word "UNSAFE". Every lime block carries ✓. Roughly 8 % of men have some colour vision deficiency, and your users are largely men working outdoors.
    Sunlight legibility. Flat high-contrast fills survive glare; soft shadows and low-contrast greys do not. This is a genuine functional advantage of the style for a field app.
    Respect prefers-reduced-motion. The translate-on-press is charming and must be disabled when asked.
    Every icon paired with text. Icon-only navigation fails with low-literacy users. Icon + word + optional audio.
    Audio-first Kisan mode. A speaker button on every card that reads the advice aloud in the selected language. For many users this is the primary channel, not an accessibility add-on.

Demo script that wins

Three cases, ninety seconds each. (1) Type "aloo kaata hua do din pehle" in Hindi → the system refuses to recommend a bag, explains the botulinum and spoilage risk, and gives actionable next-time advice. That refusal is your differentiator; lead with it. (2) Business mode, besan namkeen, 6 months → three structures ranked, and the engine picks the cheaper mono-material PP over the "better" PET/VMPET, showing EPR liability in rupees. (3) Expert mode → open the same result, change storage temperature from 30 °C to 4 °C, and watch the limiting mechanism flip from microbial to moisture live, with the equations visible. Most teams demo a form and a result. Demo a system that reasons, refuses, and shows its work.

11.3 · What will go wrong

    Unit conversion bugs. OTR in cc/100in²·day versus cc/m²·day differs by 15.5×. Adopt pint on day one and never store a bare float.
    Over-trusting vendor datasheets. Published film values are best-case, unflexed, and often at 0 % RH. EVOH and nylon barrier collapses at high humidity. Store the test conditions alongside the value or the number is meaningless.
    Optimistic shelf-life predictions. Every simplification pushes in the optimistic direction. Apply an explicit, documented safety factor (0.7–0.8 on predicted life) and show it in the ledger rather than hiding it inside a constant.
    The pretty-but-useless dashboard. Resist charts in Kisan mode. A farmer needs one instruction, not a visualisation of gas permeability.
    Scope creep into blockchain. A hash-chained Postgres table gives you tamper evidence in forty lines. Spend the saved time on the knowledge base, which is where the actual value lives.
    Regulatory staleness. The 2026 draft amendment on PFAS and BPA shows how fast this moves. Put a last_verified date on every rule and surface rules older than six months on an admin dashboard.

A note on the numbers in this document

Film barrier values, respiration rates and MAP targets given here are design-stage defaults compiled from public sources. They are appropriate for building and demonstrating the engine. They are not a substitute for a supplier certificate of analysis, a validation study, or migration testing for a commercial product. Build the provenance system described in section 8 so that this distinction is visible to every user, on every number, all the time.
